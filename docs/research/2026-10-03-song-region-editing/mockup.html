Song region editing

The Song tab's timeline: the harmony track and three part lanes, 16 bars of 4/4. Proposed is the change; Today is how it works on main, for comparison. Mouse and touch both work. Alt-click splits, and Shift while dragging snaps to the finer grid.

Proposed

  1. + halves the selected chord at its middle bar (its middle beat under two bars). With no chord selected it halves the rightmost chord, so on a new song the first + splits the song 50/50. The new half copies the chord and is selected, ready for the harmony card.
  2. The line between two chords drags. One chord grows and the other shrinks; nothing else moves. Today a drag on a chord's right edge pushes every later chord along, and the last chord takes the difference.
  3. Chord boundaries snap to bars, Shift for beats, as part regions do. A chord is at least a beat. Alt-click splits a chord where you click.
  4. Edges show themselves. Over an edge the cursor becomes a resize arrow and an amber bar lights on the edge you'll grab. The grab zone is 10 px, at most a third of the block (8 px and a quarter today). A selected block keeps its handles faintly lit, so a touch screen shows where to grab.
  5. Where two part regions touch, the seam drags both. Today that drag can only shrink one of them. Try Bass at bar 5 or Pad at bar 9.
  6. A readout while dragging: the start and end as bar.beat, and the length.
  7. Drag across an empty stretch to draw a region of that length. A click still makes one bar.

Decided (tacowars, 2026-10-03)

Approved as drawn, layout Proposed. Record 2026-10-03-song-region-editing.

  • + with no chord selected halves the rightmost chord.
  • The new half copies the chord, as + does today.
  • Part seams drag both regions by default, as drawn. No modifier.
  • The song's first and last bar fix the outer edges of the harmony track. Song length stays its own setting.

Stand-ins

Delete and Undo in the toolbar (dashed) stand in for the harmony card's and the rail's Delete and for Cmd+Z, which also works here. Region labels are examples.
