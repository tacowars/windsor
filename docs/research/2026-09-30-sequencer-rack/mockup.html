Height

  • The tallest need is a step column with a mod lane: step number, note, Oct, the A and S flags, and a lane you can paint. Grid and Arp both have one.
  • With one lane shown at a time, 196 px is enough. The Grid's roll gets 67 px (9.6 px a degree, room for a note name) and the lane 58 px. So the sequencer can share the inserts' height and the pane stays even.
  • Stacked lanes are what break it. Two lanes at 196 leave 6.4 px a degree, and four would be unusable at any of these heights. One lane at a time, with dots to switch, keeps the height fixed.
  • 220 px is the choice if lanes should stack two deep, or for more room to drag a note.

What changes

  • Grid: the note cell and the degree picker become a roll. Drag a note up or down to change its degree, and click it to cycle note → tie → rest. A tie draws as the held note carried on. Steps shrink from 46 px to 26 px, so 32 steps fit in about 900 px.
  • Arp: the same roll, but read-only in pitch. It draws the walk Style takes over the chord, so a Style or Octaves change is visible at once.
  • Chord: a step is a block as long as it plays (Dur × Rep × base step), split at each repeat. The region's chords from the Harmony tab run above, so each hit shows the chord it will play.
  • Euclid: the figure is a circle, with the density modulator drawn as the path k takes between min and max.
  • Reg on every pitched sequencer. The Song pane's separate Octave knob for Grid and Chord moves into the device.

Open questions

  • Height. 196 for both, with one lane shown at a time, or 220 for sequencers so two lanes can stack?
  • Grid's roll. A roll changes how a degree is picked: a drag instead of a list. A folded degree (past the scale) would show as a red outline on the block.
  • Basslead preview NEW needs the engine to render a region's next bars without playing them. Without it, Basslead is a narrow device with no picture.
  • Chord's harmony strip NEW is read-only. Should a click on it select that chord in the Harmony lane?
  • Hints. Today each card ends in a paragraph. Here the paragraph moves into the ? on the rail.
