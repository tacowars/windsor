Colour

Part colours

Each part gets a colour of its own when it is made, and keeps it. The colour appears wherever the console names that part: its chip in the part strip, its number tab, and its region blocks. Everything else keeps today's accents.

Decisions

  1. The colour is stored on the part, as an index into the palette. It is not worked out from the part's place in the list, so removing or reordering a part never recolours the others. Try − Remove selected: the number tabs renumber, the colours stay. A song saved without colours gets them in palette order on load. The field is additive, so no format bump.
  2. A new part takes the colour used least in the song. Ties go to the earlier colour in the palette, so a fresh song runs down the palette in order. In a song coloured by assignment alone, a removed part's colour comes back first, and with 14 colours and up to 16 parts at most two colours repeat. After the picker has doubled colours up, the next part still takes the least-used colour, ties to the earlier one. Try + Add part after a removal.
  3. The console's five accents are reserved: amber, teal, violet, rose and orange-red never become part colours. The first pass's Lavender, Indigo, Mauve, Teal, Orchid, Ochre and Tangerine sat within 12 CIEDE2000 of one of them and are dropped. Aqua, Lime, Grass, Olive, Raspberry and Frost come from Ableton Live's swatch picker, chosen to sit as far as possible from the reserved accents and from the colours kept. The closest kept colours are Blush (12.4 from rose), Magenta (12.8 from rose) and Periwinkle (14.3 from violet).
  4. The palette's order spreads neighbours apart. Each entry sits as far as the palette allows from the three before it, so parts made one after another never look alike.
  5. A chip's colour is picked by right-clicking it, or long-pressing it on touch. A 7 × 2 swatch grid opens under the chip with the part's colour ringed, and the name of the colour under the pointer below it. One click picks and closes it; Escape or a click outside closes it.
  6. The selected chip fills with its own colour, where today it fills amber. The outline around the selected part's block and lane takes its colour too.
  7. The selected region's ring is ink, where today it is amber.
  8. The region's ⟲ glyph takes the part's colour, where today it is always amber.
  9. Group folders keep their Mixer accent: teal, violet, amber, then round again.
  10. The sequencer device keeps its accent by kind (teal or amber, --kc). Its knobs and cells use the console's role colours.
  11. Drums versus melodic by colour goes. The chip still names the kind (3 · Euclid).
