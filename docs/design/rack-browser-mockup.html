Rack browser mockup

For tacowars to approve   windsor#875 · record docs/log/2026-10-17-racks.md, decisions 13, 18, 19 and 20.

A rack is a part's sound saved as one library object: its patch, its insert chain, and when asked its low cut and its sequencer. This page shows where racks appear in the console: the one browser with a Kind facet, the info pane for a rack, the patch bar's rack tag and its ⋯ menu, the Song tab's two entry points, and the Save as rack… modal. Switch the Kind facet above to compare the browser as it is today with the browser on Racks; the frame, the sizes and the type are the same.

The example racks are built from two built-in patches. Kick Drive is the EFM Kick through Classic Drive and a bus compressor keyed from another part, with a Reverb switched off, a low cut at 45 Hz and the Euclid pattern at its defaults. Dub Hat is the EFM Closed Hat through Dub delay and Filter, with no low cut and no sequencer in the rack. Today's browser is reproduced with the search on efm, the same eleven rows in both states' counts. The part plays the Euclid pattern with a low cut at 45 Hz in both of its states: the sequencer and the low cut are the part's, whether a rack set them or not, and the no-rack state is the part Kick Drive was saved from.

2The patch bar: the rack tag and the ⋯ menu

While the selected part has a rackSource, the patch box gains a RACK tag before the name, on the one line the box has today, so the bar's height does not change (settled by tacowars: a second line made the bar taller, a box of its own cost its width). The name stays the patch's own, which is the rack's right after a load (decision 6), so one name serves both; a song copy renamed later keeps its own name beside the tag, whose title names the rack. The tag carries the rack's dot, lit when the part's patch, chain, low cut or sequencer differs from the library rack, the sequencer compared without its seed, the chain compared with its external sidechain keys cleared as the file writes them (decision 3: a rack names no part, so a part keyed from another part is clean against the rack it was saved from), and a member the rack lacks never counting (decision 14, rackDifferences), so it is unlit right after a load or a save. A rackSource whose rack the library no longer holds (deleted, or on another browser) reads as no rack: no tag, no Save rack or Revert rack, no rack guard; the link stays on the part, so a rack of that id restored brings them back. The dot after the name stays the patch's own, lit by the patch's modified rule over patchSource alone and never by a chain, low cut or sequencer edit. Both rules leave the patch's name out of the comparison (settled by tacowars, item 5 below): a name is metadata Save writes anyway, so a dot means a sound edit, and the rack's dot is unlit right after a load or a Save as rack…. The patch's dot keeps its own answer through both: unlit when the part's patch is its library patch in sound, as in the frame, whose rack was saved from the unedited EFM Kick, and lit when the rack was saved over unsaved knob edits, since the embedded patch keeps its patchSource and still differs from it, before the save and after a later load. Switch the part above to see the box with and without the tag; the frame draws the rack state right after the load, with neither dot. The ⋯ menu gains a rack group after Delete: Save as rack… always, Save rack and Revert rack only in the rack state.

A click on + adds a part as today. A right-click or a long press on it opens this two-row menu; Add part from rack… opens the browser on Racks with the new part as the target, and the part is added when a rack is loaded, never before, so a Close adds nothing.

4Save as rack…

The metadata modal, as Save as… opens it, with the title and the hint changed and two checkboxes between the description and the buttons. It is drawn for the part in its no-rack state: it plays the EFM Kick with the Euclid pattern and a low cut at 45 Hz, the state Kick Drive was saved from, so both boxes come checked. The name is prefilled from the patch's name and becomes the rack's and the embedded patch's (decision 13), so on the rack-loaded part it would prefill Kick Drive; the category and the tags come from the patch's library entry; the id is uniqueId(slug(name), rack ids), so a second EFM Kick rack is efm-kick-2.

Save as rack…

Saves this part's patch and effects as a new rack in your library. The part keeps playing what it plays and follows the rack.

Checking loudness…

Save as rack… keeps Save as…'s loudness line under the description, run on the embedded patch as it plays: a rack can be saved from Init or over unsaved knob edits, so the patch may never have been checked before.

5To decide

Settled by tacowars on 2026-10-17 (PR windsor#876), and drawn above as settled:

  1. The + gesture. A click adds a part as today; a right-click or a long press opens the two-row menu with Add part from rack….
  2. The rack table's Chain column. Chain beside Tags, as drawn.
  3. Where the rack shows in the bar. Neither a second line (it made the bar taller) nor a box of its own (it cost the bar's width): a RACK tag on the box's one line, carrying the rack's dot, the name the patch's own. Recorded as decision 19.
  4. Source on Racks. The three Source rows stay, This song reading 0, since a rack is never a song object (decision 1).
  1. The name leaf in the two dots. Today's patch rule compares every leaf of the song copy against its patchSource, the name included, so a part just loaded from Kick Drive would show the patch's dot at once (its copy is named Kick Drive, its source EFM Kick); and Save as rack… under a new name writes that name into the rack's embedded patch but not into the song patch, so the rack's dot would light the moment the rack is saved. Settled: both comparisons leave name out, isModified for the patch's dot and rackDifferences for the rack's, everywhere, not only on rack-loaded parts. A dot means a sound edit; a renamed song copy loses its dot too, Rename… being a label change. Recorded in decision 14; issue D builds it.
