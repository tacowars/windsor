What it draws

  1. A Drum part named Snare in the Arp's place, in the same pane and rack: the part chip, the region's summary (drum · 16 steps · 1/16, windsor#948 decision 9) and the pane's header are rewritten over today's pixels in the app's fonts. The Chance lane in the part's lane row is the song's seq.probability lane, reading 50% at bar 1, so the device's Chance knob is drawn held by it (its AUTO tag and the lane's pink), as a held knob is today.
  2. The device is a rack device, 244 px high, laid out as the Arp: the shared rail (the dot in the Drum's perc accent, Drum, the region 1/1, Split, Delete), then Play, 12 px in from the rail, two 112 px field columns 13 px apart and the knob strip behind its rule, then Steps. Every control is the app's own markup, laid out by the app's own rules (copied from console.css as the app applies them); the Drum adds only the rules marked new in this file.
  3. Play. Column 1: Pattern, Rate, Note. Column 2: Seed with Reseed, Randomize, Reverse. The knob strip: Length and Rotate (the Grid's pair); Vel, Acc vel, Acc mod; Gate, Vary, Chance; then the toggle column, Per effect level with Gate, Rests sound with Vary and Octave with Chance, exactly as the Arp draws it. Gate moves from the issue's first group into the Arp's column, so the toggles sit beside the knobs they shape exactly as on the Arp, and the first column holds only fields. No Octave register knob: the pitch is Note.
  4. Pattern is the insert cards' preset picker (presetInsertCard.ts: a select.field, Custom first), grouped Kick, Snare, Clap, Hat, Open hat, Perc. It shows the pattern the steps and the length equal, or Custom. The example reads Custom, since its ratchet and its pitch offsets leave every pattern; pick Snare, Ghost backbeat, to see the same line without them. A choice replaces the steps and the length and nothing else (the step lane's values stay). The names and lines in it here stand in for the bank, which 3/4 writes.
  5. Note is a stepper, the Euclid's Note box (euclidStepper.ts: − C4 · 60 +, a semitone a press), at the field column's width. Why not a knob: the Euclid, the other drum device, already sets a drum part's base note with this box, so a Snare reads the same on both; a press lands on a semitone exactly, where a knob over 0 to 127 needs a fine drag; and it fits the field column, where a knob would need a slot of its own.
  6. Steps. A column per step at the Grid's 32 px, 2 px apart as the Grid's (the Arp's are 6), each beat set off by the meter's 4 px and each bar by 10 (meterGrid.ts); steps past Length greyed as the Grid's (drag Length down to see it, up to add rests to 32). Rows, top down: the step number; the hit, a square pad 32 × 32 px (the step's width), the device's main control; the Vel bar; Pitch; A; the ratchet at 14 px. The other cells are the Arp's 20 px with its 4 px row gap, so the held rows are 138 px, 12 more than Basslead's 126. What gives is the lane area: under the 244 px device the strip is 218 px high, so two 42 px step lanes showed in full before the lanes scroll and now one does, with 34 px of the second (the Arp shows one and 22 px). A tighter row gap would need 1.6 px in place of 4 to win the 12 px back. Then + Lane and the lane count in the corner, the Ratchet label, and one step lane (Op A Decay: the ghosts shorter, the backbeat longer) at 42 px. The playhead ring is on step 5.
  7. The hit cell: a square pad, a 12 px filled dot in the part's accent for a hit, an empty ring for a rest. A click toggles; a new hit plays at 1.
  8. The Vel bar is the Figure's (figureCells.ts): the value written on it (.3, .25), a fill to it, faint and unfilled at 1. A vertical drag moves it in steps of 0.05 over 80 px; a double-click puts it back to 1.
  9. Pitch is the Grid's note cell with the played note's name (note + pitch, moved by octaves into 0 to 127 as the engine plays it), the offset in its tooltip. At offset 0 it shows the part's own note, faint, as Oct is faint at 0. A vertical drag moves it a semitone per 8 px, to ±24; a double-click puts it back to the part's note.
  10. A and the ratchet are the Arp's: A lit in the accent, the ratchet ×1 to ×4 by click (three ticks on step 15), grey and inert on a rest.
  11. The glyph toggles carry today's glyphs, names and tooltips, copied word for word (Octave's reads the step's own note and stays). The example has Rests sound on.

Choices made here

  • Note as a stepper, and Gate beside Vary and Chance rather than in the first group (above).
  • The Grid's 2 px column gap. The Drum groups its steps by the meter as the Grid does; the Arp's 6 px gap is for an ungrouped cycle. It saves 60 px at 16 steps (1156 against 1216) and 124 px at 32.
  • The Chance lane. The issue lists it among the strip's lanes, but the strip holds step lanes (voice targets) only: Chance is the song's lane, in the part's lane row, where the frames draw it. The strip carries the one step lane.
  • Two tooltips that name the Arp. Vary's says on the Arp, its gate and Per effect's the Arp's gate, and the Drum's gate varies too (windsor#948 decision 5). They are the app's shared text and stay as written here; the build can say the Arp's and the Drum's gate.
  • Picker 5, the Vel bar's fill, is an addition: the issue reads the Figure's bar as filling upward (a velocity bar's height is the velocity), but the Figure's bar, and the Arp's Gate bar after it, fill across, dragged up or down. Decided: across, the real bar. Up draws the fill from the cell's foot.

Decided in the issue

The device's place and height, the rail, the controls and the rows and their order, the pattern picker's look and rule, the glyph toggles as the Arp's, and no Octave register knob (windsor#949 decisions 2 to 4, over windsor#948's fields).

Decided on PR windsor#953 (2026-10-24)

tacowars took every recommended option and made the hit cells square pads. The pickers open on the choice; the other ways stay selectable.

  1. Pitch display. Chosen: the played note's name (C4, D#4): what sounds, and a tom roll reads as notes. The other way, Offset, writes +3 and -2 (0 faint), with the note in the tooltip.
  2. Where Pattern sits. Chosen: at the head of Play, the first thing a new part needs, showing its name. The other way puts it on the rail between the name and the region, as an 18 px button (a new glyph, steps with hits) that opens the same grouped list, the chosen name in its tooltip. Neither changes the width: Rate and Note keep the field column.
  3. A ghost hit's look. Chosen: the dot dims below 0.5, so the ghosts read in the hit row. The other way keeps every dot solid and the Vel bar alone shows it.
  4. Randomize's rule (its tooltip; the button works). Chosen: the hit count kept, the hits at new places, each at a velocity from 0.3 to 1. The other way draws 8 of 16 (half the Length) at the same velocities. Here a new hit has no accent, pitch or ratchet; the build's ticket says what Randomize does to those.
  5. The Vel bar's fill (an addition, above). Chosen: across, the Figure's bar as it is; the other way fills up.
