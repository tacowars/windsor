Bigger arrows, and a mixer without numbers

Two changes to the Song tab's frozen column. Everything below is drawn at the console's real sizes (the 40 px part row, the 50 px fold column, the 26 px compact dial). Click the arrows, and drag the knobs up and down.

Approved by tacowars, 2026-10-12

Arrows: option C. Draw the arrow as a shape that fills its box, and make the box the whole empty cell around it: the 50 × 40 px fold column on a part row, and arrow plus "Mixer" in the header. No new space is used.

Values: option A. No numbers beside any pot. While you hover a knob, or as soon as you press it, a bubble above it shows the parameter's name and value, Low cut in Hz. Expanded, the mixer column drops from 485 px to 315 px and the timeline gains 170 px, about 1.8 bars at the default zoom.

1 · The arrows

A UI designer would make three points about today's arrows:

  • A font character never fills its box. ▸ sits on a text baseline with side bearings, so a 12 px glyph draws as about 6 px of ink. In the panel face it can come out as a dot, which is why the part fold already falls back to the system font. An SVG shape can run to the box's edges, and looks the same in every font and browser.
  • The target is the box, and the box can grow into free space. The fold column is 50 × 40 px, but its button is 17 × 18 px. By Fitts's law a bigger target is quicker to hit, which matters on touch. It costs no layout space.
  • The label belongs to the target. In the header, "Mixer" next to the arrow should be clickable too, as in a disclosure triangle in Finder or Logic's track list.

2 · Mixer values

Today every pot carries its value to its right, which makes each expanded column 70 px wide. With the numbers gone, a column only needs the 26 px dial plus a gap, so I've used 36 px. The readout has to go somewhere else:

  • Show the value at the control, while it moves. The eye is on the knob while you turn it, so that is where the number should appear. A readout somewhere else on screen makes you look away mid-gesture.
  • Above, not beside. On touch the finger covers the knob and whatever sits to either side of it, so the readout goes above. That is the pattern of a phone keyboard's key preview.
  • Say what it is. On a part three rows down, the column label is far away, so the bubble names the parameter: "Low cut 120 Hz".
  • Reading without moving. Hovering for a moment shows the value without changing it. On touch, a press shows it straight away.
