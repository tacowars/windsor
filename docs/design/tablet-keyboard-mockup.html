Mockup In a frame, Expand, Close, Esc, Oct − / +, Octaves, Rows and the part chips are live.

What changes

  1. The keys row on a touch screen (@media (pointer: coarse)): white keys of equal width, never under 44 px, the black keys over them at 60 % of a white key's width and 62 % of the row's height, centred on the line between their two white keys. The row is 110 px high (46 px today). Two octaves, as today: 14 white keys, so the keys need 616 px. Where the row leaves less beside the buttons, the buttons wrap under the keys (portrait, the phone). The colours are today's.
  2. Expand, the one new button on the keys row, on every device, at the end of the button group: a .btn with the roll's rail icon (rollExpand.ts's expand path) and the word. At 1440 × 900 with a mouse nothing else changes; the keys give Expand's width to it.
  3. The expanded keyboard opens below the live header, 16 px under it and 24 px from the sides and bottom, on the ground colour, like the roll and the workbench (#855, #856). Its bar: the title (the family's, "Keys — Keyboard"), Oct −, the octave label, Oct +, Octaves 1 · 2 · 3 · 4, Rows 1 · 2, Hold, Panic and Close. The keys fill what is left; two rows sit 12 px apart, the lower octaves on the lower row. The C of each octave shows its name. The part chips stay live: pick Pad and the keyboard plays Pad.
  4. A disabled option keeps its place and says why in its title. On the portrait tablet, 4 octaves in one row would make a white key 34.9 px, so it is disabled at 1 row ("4 octaves needs 2 rows at this width"); Rows 2 is disabled for 1 and 3 octaves ("2 rows takes 2 or 4 octaves"), and with 2 rows chosen, 1 and 3 octaves are disabled ("3 octaves can't split into 2 rows"). With 4 octaves chosen in portrait, Rows 1 is disabled by the width.
  5. The strike velocity (touch and pen): 0.6 at a key's top edge, 0.8 at its middle, 1.0 at its bottom edge, linear, a black key over its own height. A mouse keeps 0.9. The "Strike velocity" frame draws the zones on E and F♯; the app draws nothing.
  6. The phone (below 720 px): one octave on the keys row, 7 white keys at 51.7 px, the buttons under it. The expanded view is the way to play more.

The numbers

The room for the keys is the window less the view's padding: 24 px each side, so 1318 px in landscape and 976 px in portrait. Its height is the window less the 127 px header, the 16 px above the bar, the 28 px bar, the 8 px under it and the 24 px at the bottom: 821 px in landscape, 1163 px in portrait. Two rows split it with a 12 px gap. A white key's width is the room's width over the white keys in a row; red is under 44 px, disabled.

The octave label names the lowest C as the keyboard does today (C${octave}, MIDI note = 12 × octave, so C4 is 48). Oct + stops where the highest key is still in the engine's range 0–127: at C8 the 2-octave keyboard tops out at B9 (119), at C7 three octaves do, at C6 four. On the 110 px touch row a white key reads 0.6 at its top, 0.8 at 55 px and 1.0 at 110 px; a black key (68.2 px) 0.6, 0.8 at 34.1 px and 1.0 at 68.2 px.

To decide

  1. The touch row's height on the Parts tab. Recommended: 110 px. Room for the strike velocity to spread (0.4 over 110 px) and for a finger to land on a black key's lower half, and still short enough that the Parts tab's cards keep most of the screen.
  2. The black keys' height on the touch row. Recommended: 62 % (68.2 px of 110). It leaves 41.8 px of white below every black key, just under the 44 px touch row size, because a finger landing between black keys has the whole white key's width as well.
  3. The expanded view's default. Recommended: 2 octaves, 1 row: 94.1 px white keys in landscape and 69.7 px in portrait, the same two octaves as the Parts row.
  4. Expand on the desktop. Recommended: yes. The view is as useful with a mouse, the button (91.1 px and an 8 px gap) costs the keys 99.2 px of today's 996.7 px at 1440 × 900, a white key going from 46.7 to 41.8 px, and one keys row for every device is less to build and test.
  5. Note names on the expanded keys. Recommended: yes, the C of each octave only, in the octave label's naming, so the bar and the keys agree. That naming calls MIDI 48 C4, where the rest of the console (noteName, the roll's keys) calls 60 C4; the record lists one naming for both as Later. The touch row on the Parts tab stays unlabelled, as today.
