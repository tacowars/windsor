Today

  • Opens fitted to the region: a short loop in a long region is a row of slivers.
  • ↔ reads fit or px/bar; ↕ reads px/row. Neither says what you see.
  • fit sits between − and +, so a near miss on either jumps back out to the whole region.
  • The ruler does nothing on a drag, though the Song tab's ruler zooms.

A · Musical steppers · not chosen

  • The Zoom item stays, rebuilt: Loop and Region fit buttons on their own row, then ↔ and ↕ steppers whose middles are readouts, not buttons.
  • ↔ halves or doubles what is shown, so it lands on round values: 2 bars, 1 bar, 2 beats. ↕ reads octaves shown.
  • + Every action has a visible button; nearest to today; nothing to misclick.
  • − Uses three rows of column 2. Doesn't show where you are in a 50-bar region.

B · Zoom bars · chosen

  • The scrollbars become zoom handles: drag an end to zoom, the middle to scroll, click the track to jump there.
  • The ↔ bar is a map of the whole region: the loop is marked and the notes are ticks, so you always see where you are in 50 bars. The ↕ bar maps the pitches.
  • Column 2 keeps only Loop and Region.
  • + Visible, direct, and the one design that shows position. Works the same with a finger (the bars grow on the iPad).
  • − Takes 4 px more from the notes than today's scrollbars. The ends are small targets on a mouse.

C · Gestures only · not chosen

  • Zoom leaves the controls. The ruler and the keys do it all, a readout floats while you drag, and the summary names the bars shown.
  • Double-click the ruler to switch between the loop and the region.
  • Column 2 has room for more.
  • + Most room, one way to zoom, the same gesture as the Song tab.
  • − Nothing on screen says you can zoom until you try. Tooltips don't exist on the iPad.

In A, B and C

  1. The roll opens on the loop. A short loop in a long region fills the width, and its repeats carry on to the right under the hatch. Double-click the ruler, or press Region, for the whole region; press Loop to come back.
  2. The ruler zooms like the Song tab's. Drag up to zoom in, down to zoom out, around the bar you pressed; drag left and right to scroll.
  3. The keys zoom rows. Drag them right for taller rows, left for shorter, up and down to scroll. A tap still plays the key.
  4. Pinch zooms around your fingers. On a trackpad (or Ctrl/⌘ + wheel) it zooms time. On the iPad, spreading sideways zooms time and spreading up and down zooms rows; one finger on empty space still scrolls and a tap still adds a note (the Roll's record, decision 8).
  5. No px anywhere. Time reads in bars or beats, rows in octaves.

On the iPad

  • Pick an iPad screen above: the rack takes the iPad Pro 12.9″'s width, and the zoom bars take their touch size, as they will whenever any pointer is coarse.
  • The bars grow from 12 to 22 px, and each end is a 26 × 22 px grip with two ridges. The thumb never gets shorter than 84 px, so both grips and a middle to hold stay under a finger even at the region's full width.
  • The device view gives the notes 10 px less than on the desktop. The Expanded view has the room, and opens on the loop at 15 px rows, as today.
  • The readout that floats while you zoom is larger, and it sits at the top of the notes, clear of your fingers.
  • Touch can't be tried in a desktop browser: pinch and one-finger pans need the iPad.

Decided (tacowars, 2026-10-18)

  • Option B, the zoom bars, with everything under "In A, B and C".
  • Duplicate (Dup beside the Loop stepper) doubles the loop and copies everything in it into the new half: the notes with their velocity, accents and ratchets, and the loop's envelopes. It stops at the region's length.
  • The closest zoom rises from 120 to 240 px per beat (a 1/16 is 60 px wide).
  • Double-click on the ruler switches between the loop and the region.
  • The Expanded view gets option B too, and keeps its own zoom, separate from the device's, as today.
